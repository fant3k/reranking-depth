# Does more router training data help?

The original frozen test already failed the quality margin. This separate follow-up asks whether the size of the router's labeled training pool is a plausible limitation. It was specified after that result and before scoring or inspecting additional training-pool queries. The original test remains exploratory for this follow-up.

Use previously unused US-English ESCI training queries with at least twenty candidates. Exclude normalized text already in the original Amazon training/calibration sample, WANDS, or the official ESCI test. Remove normalized collisions in the additional pool. Select at most 4,800 eligible queries deterministically by hash. Use a different salted hash to reserve up to 800 additional queries as a previously unevaluated replication holdout. Leave at least half of the additional pool available for fitting. A holdout from the official training partition has limited external validity.

Combine the remaining additions with the original 1,600 fitting queries. Each of five seeds yields one permutation of this enlarged training set. Fit the fixed pre and post random forests on nested prefixes of 100, 400, 1,600, 3,200, and the full available size (omit unavailable or repeated sizes). Use the original 200 calibration queries to set the median score threshold throughout. Keep the same estimator settings, paired inputs and nDCG definition. Compare both test sets, all seeds and sample sizes. Report differences from fixed depth twenty, from a random route at the same pair count in expectation, and the fraction of deep decisions. Uncertainty across held-out queries is computed over the five-seed mean difference for each query. No selection of best seed, model, or budget on either test. Queueing latency is already characterized for the fixed serving policy shapes; this follow-up does not benchmark new routing policies or certify their latency.
